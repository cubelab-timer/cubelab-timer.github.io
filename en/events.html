Events and how to choose a speedcube

What each event in CubeLab Timer is like, and what to look for when picking a puzzle to practice with.

What to look for in a competition cube

  • Magnets: layers snap into place, so you overshoot turns less. Almost every competition cube today is magnetic.
  • Stickerless: the colour is in the plastic, so there are no stickers to peel.
  • Adjustability: cubes with adjustable tension and magnet strength can be tuned to how you turn.
  • Your first good cube: many people start with a budget magnetic cube (around $10–15) and try flagships once they know what they like.
  • Lube: a drop of cube lubricant brings back smooth turning when a cube feels dry.

N×N cubes

3×3

The most popular event. Beginners usually start with a layer-by-layer method and move on to CFOP to get faster. CubeLab scrambles are random-move scrambles of about 20 moves.

Find a magnetic 3×3Find a beginner 3×3

2×2

Very few moves; top solvers finish in a couple of seconds. Much of what you know from 3×3 carries over, so it is a great second event.

Find a 2×2

4×4 and 5×5

The usual approach is reduction: solve the centers, pair the edges, then finish like a 3×3. On 4×4, watch out for parity cases.

6×6 and 7×7

At competitions these use a mean of 3 (mo3) instead of an average of 5. CubeLab shows mo3 in your stats as well.

3×3 variations

One-Handed (OH)

Solve a 3×3 with one hand. Light, fast-turning cubes are popular.

Blindfolded (BLD)

Memorize the cube, put on a blindfold and solve it; the time includes memorization. The wide moves (Rw, Uw) at the end of the scramble randomize the orientation.

Other events

Pyraminx

A tetrahedron with few moves, so beginners improve quickly. Lowercase moves (u, l, r, b) turn only the tips.

Find a Pyraminx

Skewb

A cube that turns around its corners. It needs fewer moves than it looks and is a fan favourite.

Find a Skewb

Megaminx

A twelve-sided dodecahedron solved with 3×3 ideas. Scrambles use their own notation, such as “R++ D--”.

Find a Megaminx

Square-1

A shape-shifting puzzle: first return it to a cube shape, then solve the colours. Scrambles combine top/bottom turns like “(1,0)” with slashes “/”.

Find a Square-1

Clock

Nine clocks on each side. Use the pins and dials to set every hand to 12 o'clock.

Find a Clock

CTO (Corner-Turning Octahedron)

An octahedron whose corners turn in 90° steps. It is not an official WCA event but appears at some unofficial competitions. In CubeLab, uppercase moves (U, R, F, D, L, B) are the big turns and lowercase moves turn only the tips.
